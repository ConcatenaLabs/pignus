Open offers

A funded offer holds the lender's principal in a covenant, so you can take it with the lender offline. Taking one locks your collateral in a vault whose address the offer's own script recomputes from your key — it cannot pay anyone else, and it cannot be pointed anywhere else.

Publish a funded offer

Your principal goes into an offer covenant with the terms baked in. Borrowers can take it while you are offline; you cannot oversell it, and you cannot change a term afterwards. Anything untaken returns to you after the expiry — permissionlessly, to your pinned address, so you need no key beyond the one your wallet already has.

You will see the exact terms and the offer address before your wallet asks you to sign.

Check a repurchase Tier D — not a loan

A regulated (OpenDAMP) asset cannot be locked in a lending vault: the network requires every output carrying it to pay the holder's own covenant address, exits cannot be pre-signed because the shared verifier output moves on any holder's transfer, and the issuer has no power to move a coin either. So on default nobody could ever reach the collateral, and a loan against one is not something that can be built. What can be built is a repurchase: you SELL the asset to the lender, and their obligation to sell it back is secured by a bond in a covenant here.

Settling one needs the regulated asset's own machinery, which a browser does not have, so the transaction is composed elsewhere. What this page does is the part that protects you: rebuild the bond vault's address from the terms you were given and check it against the coin that is actually funded. If they match, every destination and the deadline are exactly what you agreed.

What this page does, and what it cannot do

Every vault address here is derived in your browser from the terms shown, and compared to what is actually on chain before anything is signed. That check is the whole of the non-custodial claim, and it is why this page refuses to build a transaction when an offer's address does not match its terms rather than letting your wallet ask you about it.

It cannot move your money: it holds no keys, and the wallet shows you its own reading of every transaction before you approve it. If the two disagree, reject it — and tell us.

Prefer the command line, or want to run this against your own node? The CLI does everything this page does: pignus-cli, on the downloads page.
