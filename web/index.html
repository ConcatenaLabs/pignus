Pignus lending

Borrow USDX against GOLD, SILVR, OILX, tSEQ or native Bitcoin. The loan's terms are compiled into a covenant and enforced by the script interpreter, so there is no custodian, no term that can be changed after the loan opens, and no permission needed to repay.

Prices signed by the oracle, verified here before display

A price is quoted as debt-asset atoms per collateral-asset atom. Every one of these carries a signature over the exact numbers a covenant would compute with, and this page checks that signature before showing it.

Before you sign anything

Everything Pignus claims reduces to one check, and it is a check you run, not one you are told has been run. The loan terms and the vault address are the same fact stated twice, so rebuilding the address from the terms you agreed proves the terms have not been altered — by a counterparty, by a book, or by this page.

  1. pignus-cli verify --terms loan.json --txid <funding> --vout 0
  2. It refuses if a single atom of the debt, the strike, the maturity, the lender, the oracle or the market differs from what you agreed.
  3. It also checks the vault has no key path, so the four exits really are the only ways the collateral can move.

Offers

A funded offer has the lender's principal already locked in a covenant: anyone can take it, and the lender need not be online. A signed offer is a quote, and the lender must co-sign when you take it.

Loans

Health is the current price divided by the strike: below 1.00 a position can be liquidated by anyone, and the covenant forces the surplus back to the borrower whoever does it.

How a loan ends

Repay — permissionless, needs no oracle and no signature at all. A solvent borrower can always leave.

Liquidate — permissionless once the oracle attests a price under the strike. The seizure is computed on chain and the surplus is forced home.

Default — the same, at any price, once the term is up.

Recover — the lender's backstop, long after maturity, so a dead oracle cannot freeze collateral for ever.

What is trusted

The oracle can assert a price low enough to open a liquidation. That is the whole of its power: it cannot move funds, choose a recipient, change how much is seized, call a loan early, stop a repayment, or keep a borrower's surplus. Every attestation it has ever made is published, so a fabricated price is permanently visible next to the real ones.

A vault may name several independent oracles with a threshold, in which case no single one can trigger a liquidation and no single one can block it.
